Male Hypogonadism

Androgen Insufficiency Symptoms

  • Incomplete or delayed sexual development
  • Low libido
  • Decreased spontaneous erections
  • Gynecomastia
  • Loss of body (axillary and pubic) hair, reduced shaving
  • Very small or shrinking testes
  • Inability to father children, low or zero sperm count
  • Height loss, low-trauma fracture, low bone mineral density
  • Hot flushes, sweats
  • Decreased energy, motivation, initiative, and self-confidence
  • Feeling sad or blue, depressed mood, dysthymia
  • Poor concentration and memory
  • Sleep disturbance, increased sleepiness
  • Mild anemia (normochromic, normocytic, in the female range)
  • Reduced muscle bulk and strength
  • Increased body fat, body mass index
  • Diminished physical or work performance

Causes of Low Sex Hormone Binding Globulin

Note: SHBG binds testosterone, makes it less bioavailable but makes bound testosterone remain in circulation longer.

Note: Total testosterone = Free_testosterone + Albumin-bound_testosterone + SHBG-bound_testosterone

  • Obesity/Overweight
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Exogenous androgens/anabolic steroids/some progestins
  • Glucocorticoids
  • Growth hormone excess
  • Hypothyroidism
  • Nephrotic syndrome
  • SHBG gene polymorphisms

Risks of Testosterone Treatment

  • Erythrocytosis
  • Obstructive Sleep Apnea
  • Bladder Outlet Obstruction
  • Peripheral Edema
  • Hair Loss
  • Acne
  • Heard Disease
  • Blood Clots

Contraindications to Testosterone Treatment

  • Breast cancer
  • Elevated hematocrit (>48%)
  • Fertility planning
  • Myocardial infarction or stroke within the past 6 months
  • Prostate cancer or palpable prostate nodule or induration
  • PSA >3 ng/mL in men at increased risk of prostate cancer (e.g., African American men or men with a first-degree relative with prostate cancer) without urologic evaluation
  • PSA >4 ng/mL without urologic evaluation
  • Severe lower urinary tract symptoms
  • Thrombophilia (hypercoagulable disorder)
    • Factor V Leiden mutation
    • Prothrombin G20210A mutation
    • Protein C deficiency
    • Protein S deficiency
    • Antithrombin deficiency
    • Antiphospholipid syndrome
    • Myeloproliferative neoplasms (e.g., polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia)
  • Uncontrolled congestive heart failure
  • Untreated severe obstructive sleep apnea

Causes of Secondary Hypogonadism

  • Pituitary tumors
  • Prolactinoma
  • Nonfunctioning macroadenoma
  • Craniopharyngioma
  • Hemochromatosis
  • Cushing's Syndrome
  • Kallman's Syndrome (causes anosmia)
  • Untreated sleep apnea
  • Chronic narcotic use
  • Idiopathic

Findings Suggestive of Pituitary Abnormality

  • Visual field abnormality
  • Persistent headache
  • Intracranial nerve abnormality
  • Low T4 with normal TSH
  • Low 8 AM cortisol and Low ACTH
  • Polyuria: >3L urine/24hrs OR >50 mL/kg/day
  • Polydipsia: >3L intake/day

Common Alkylating Agents

  • Cyclophosphamide
  • Ifosfamide
  • Busulfan
  • Melphalan
  • Chlorambucil
  • Bendamustine
  • Temozolomide
  • Carmustine
  • Lomustine
  • Procarbazine
  • Thiotepa
  • Cisplatin (alkylating-like)
  • Carboplatin (alkylating-like)
  • Oxaliplatin (alkylating-like)

Treatment

Monitoring

  • CBC: at start, 3 months, every 12 months, and 3 months after any dose adjustment
    • Hold TRT if Hct ≥54%, restart and decrease TRT at 3 month mark
    • Decrease TRT if Hct 50-54%
  • PSA: at start, 3 months, and 12 months.
    • Refer to urology if PSA ↑ ≥1.4 ng/mL above baseline
    • Check contraindications if PSA ≥ 3.0 ng/mL
  • DRE: at start, 3 months, and 12 months.
    • Hold TRT and refer to urology if nodule present
  • Total Testosterone: Non-fasting, any-time-of-day, mid-cycle (half-way between injections), done 3 months after starting TRT and 3 months after any adjustment and at least every 12 months
    • Target total testosterone that is in the middle of the lab's reference range. If outside that range by more than 50 ng/d, consider dose adjustment.

Common Starting Medications

Testosterone cypionate 100 mg IM weekly Best for most patients
AndroGel 1.62% (20.25 mg/1.25 g) 2 pumps applied QAM to upper arms after showering & drying. Allow gel to air dry. Leave in place for ≥5 hours. Avoid skin contact w/women or children. Best for needle-phobes and those who are using anticoagulants who are not often in close proximity to children.
