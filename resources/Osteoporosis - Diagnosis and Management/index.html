Clinical Risk Factors for Fracture

  • Man ≥ 70 years old
  • High Fall Risk
    • Timed Up-and-Go ≥12 sec
    • ≥2 falls in past year
    • Long-term use of a walker/gait aid
  • History of fragility fracture*
  • ≥3 months of systemic prednisone-equivalent ≥5 mg/day
  • BMI <20 or weight < 58 kg
  • Parental history of hip fracture
  • Loss of height as an adult >1.5 inches
  • Hyperkyphosis (standing cobb angle ≥50°)
  • Current cigarette smoking
  • Alcohol ≥3 drinks/day
  • Inflammatory Diseases
    • Rheumatoid Arthritis
    • Inflammatory Bowel Disease
  • Hyperthyroidism
  • Primary Hyperparathyroidism
  • Hypogonadism
    • Aromatase inhibitor use (letrozole, anastrozole, exemestane)
    • Menopause @ <45 years
    • Anti-androgen therapy: leuprolide, degarelix, relugolix, or orchiectomy
  • Malabsorptive states
    • Celiac
    • Bariatric surgery, particularly gastric bypass

*Fragility Fracture: fracture from ground-level fall or less that would not have caused a fracture in most healthy persons

Labs

Initial Laboratory Evaluation

Lab Test Notes
CBC may identify anemia/cytopenias suggesting secondary disease.
CMP Calcium: Hyperparathyroidism, malignancy, or hypocalcemia.
Renal function: CKD and medication eligibility.
Alkaline phosphatase: High bone turnover, osteomalacia, Paget disease, or hepatobiliary disease.
Albumin and liver tests: Interpretation of total calcium, malnutrition, and chronic liver disease.
Serum Phosphate Hypophosphatemia may suggest vitamin D deficiency, malabsorption, hyperparathyroidism, or renal phosphate wasting. Hyperphosphatemia may occur with advanced CKD or hypoparathyroidism.
25-Hydroxyvitamin D Look for vitamin D deficiency or insufficiency contributing to secondary hyperparathyroidism, impaired calcium absorption, and osteomalacia.
TSH with Reflex Free T4 Look for overt hyperthyroidism or excessive thyroid-hormone replacement causing accelerated bone loss.
Intact PTH Look for primary hyperparathyroidism or secondary hyperparathyroidism caused by vitamin D deficiency, inadequate calcium intake, malabsorption, or CKD. Interpret with calcium, phosphate, vitamin D, and renal function.
24-Hour Urine Calcium, Sodium, Creatinine Obtain after vitamin D repletion and 2 weeks of 1200 mg daily calcium intake.
High urinary calcium: Hypercalciuria or excessive calcium loss, with possible nephrolithiasis risk.
Low urinary calcium: Low calcium intake, malabsorption, or an incomplete collection.
Urine creatinine helps assess collection completeness.
Sodium: Helpful for interpreting a high urinary calcium level.

Follow-Up Laboratory Monitoring

Lab Test Notes
BMP Usually obtained annually during treatment and before IV bisphosphonate doses.
Creatinine/eGFR: Monitor renal function and continued medication eligibility.
Calcium: Look for treatment-associated hypocalcemia or hypercalcemia from excessive supplementation or another disorder.
25-Hydroxyvitamin D Repeat every 1–2 years in patients with malabsorption, bariatric surgery, CKD, low sun exposure, obesity, poor adherence, or medications affecting vitamin D metabolism. Routine repeat testing is generally unnecessary in stable, vitamin-D-replete patients without ongoing risk factors.

General Treatment

  • Weight-bearing exercise
  • Balance training
  • Calcium intake: 1200 mg daily (supplementation + diet)
  • Vitamin D supplementation: ≥800 IU daily (higher if malabsorptive or on anti-seizure meds)
  • Quit smoking
  • Avoid heavy alcohol consumption
  • Limit glucocorticoids to lowest effective dose for shortest duration
  • ≤2 servings caffeine/day
  • Protein intake ≥0.8 g/kg/day
  • Maintain healthy weight
  • Fall prevention
    • Avoid Beer's List Meds
    • Grab bars (toilet, shower)
    • Declutter walkways
    • Avoid rugs
    • Good lighting
      • Switches at top and bottom of stairs
      • Night lights

Very High Fracture Risk

  • T-Score ≤-3.0
  • FRAX: MOF≥30% or ≥4.5% Hip
  • Fragility fracture in previous 12 months or while on osteoporosis therapy

High Fracture Risk

  • T-Score ≤-2.5
  • FRAX: MOF≥20% or ≥3% Hip
  • Prior spine or hip fracture

Oral Bisphosphonate

Contraindications

  • Achalasia
  • Esophageal strictures
  • Esophageal varices
  • History of Roux-en-Y/Gastric Bypass
  • Inability to remain upright x≥30 mins
  • Hypocalcemia

Relative Contraindications

  • Barrett's Esophagus
  • GERD
  • History of stomach ulcers
  • Difficulty remembering to use medication

Choosing a Medication For Very High Risk Patients

Medication Regimen Best Fit
Romosozumab (Evenity) 2 x 105 mg SC monthly x 12 months Best BMD gains. Most convenient. Women only. (monthly dosing). Avoid in those with history of MI, stroke, or are high cardiovascular risk. Avoid in those with upcoming invasive dental procedure (small risk of osteonecrosis of jaw)
Abaloparatide (Tymlos) 80 mcg SC daily x18-24 mo Next best choice. Low likelihood of cardiovascular events. Avoid in those with prior skeletal radiation, Paget disease, unexplained alkaline phosphatase elevation, or history of bone metastasis. No osteonecrosis risk.
Teriparatide (Forteo) 20 mcg SC daily x2 yr Avoid in those with prior skeletal radiation, Paget disease, unexplained alkaline phosphatase elevation, or history of bone metastasis. No osteonecrosis risk. Avoid in patients with renal disease

Choosing an Antiresorptive Medication

Medication Regimen Notes
Alendronate (Fosamax) 70 mg PO weekly (or 10 mg PO daily) First-line. Reduces vertebral, nonvertebral, and hip fractures. Take on empty stomach with full glass of water, remain upright ≥30 min. Avoid if CrCl <35 mL/min. Contraindicated in achalasia, esophageal stricture/Barrett, hypocalcemia. Reassess at 3–5 yr for possible drug holiday. Rare ONJ/atypical femur fracture with long-term use.
Risedronate (Actonel) 35 mg PO weekly (or 5 mg daily; 150 mg monthly) First-line alternative to alendronate. Same upright/empty-stomach rules. Slightly less restrictive renal cutoff (avoid if CrCl <30). Best CKD safety data among oral agents.
Zoledronic acid (Reclast) 5 mg IV once yearly (infuse over ≥15 min) Best choice when adherence is a concern or oral route not tolerated No rebound if next dose is late. Contraindicated if CrCl <35 mL/min - check renal function and hydrate before each dose. Acute-phase reaction (flu-like symptoms) in ~30% after first infusion, pretreat with acetaminophen.
Denosumab (Prolia) 60 mg SC every 6 months Greatest BMD gains among antiresorptives; reduces vertebral, nonvertebral, and hip fractures. Useful when bisphosphonates are contraindicated, including renal impairment (not renally cleared). Critical gotcha: do NOT delay or stop. Avoid delays; do not discontinue without a planned antiresorptive transition, usually a bisphosphonate. Check/correct vitamin D and calcium first (hypocalcemia risk, especially in CKD). Rare ONJ/atypical femur fracture.
