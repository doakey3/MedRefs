End-Organ Damage

Organ Signs/Symptoms
Brain
  • Thunderclap headache
  • Confusion
  • Focal weakness/numbness
  • Speech change
  • Seizure
Eye
  • Vision change
Heart
  • Acute dyspnea
  • Acute orthopnea
  • Chest pain
  • Radiation
  • Diaphoresis
  • Tearing chest/back pain
Kidney
  • Cola-colored urine
  • Acute lower extremity swelling

Home BP Monitoring

  • 7 Days
  • 2 readings separated by ≥1 minute twice daily (4 readings/day)
  • First reading within 1 hr of waking, after urination, before meds

Considerations for individualizing antihypertensive therapy

Indication or contraindication Antihypertensive drugs
Compelling indications (major improvement in outcome independent of blood pressure)
Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction ACE inhibitor or ARB, beta blocker, diuretic, aldosterone antagonist
Postmyocardial infarction ACE inhibitor or ARB, beta blocker, aldosterone antagonist
Proteinuric chronic kidney disease ACE inhibitor or ARB
Angina pectoris Beta blocker, calcium channel blocker
Atrial fibrillation rate control Beta blocker, nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
Atrial flutter rate control Beta blocker, nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
Hypokalemia Aldosterone antagonist
Likely to have a favorable effect on symptoms in comorbid conditions
Benign prostatic hyperplasia Alpha blocker
Essential tremor Beta blocker (noncardioselective)
Hyperthyroidism Beta blocker
Migraine Beta blocker, calcium channel blocker
Osteoporosis Thiazide diuretic
Raynaud phenomenon Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
Contraindications
Angioedema Do not use an ACE inhibitor
Bronchospastic disease Do not use a nonselective beta blocker
Liver disease Do not use methyldopa
Pregnancy (or at risk for) Do not use an ACE inhibitor, ARB, or renin inhibitor (eg, aliskiren)
Second- or third-degree heart block Do not use a beta blocker or nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker unless a functioning ventricular pacemaker is present
Drug classes that may have adverse effects on comorbid conditions
Depression Generally avoid beta blocker, central alpha-2 agonist
Gout Generally avoid loop or thiazide diuretic
Hyperkalemia Generally avoid aldosterone antagonist, ACE inhibitor, ARB, renin inhibitor
Hyponatremia Generally avoid thiazide diuretic

Study Notes
Initial evaluation
BMP Establish Na, K, Ca, creatinine, and eGFR before treatment. May reveal CKD, hyperaldosteronism, or another secondary cause and guides drug selection.
Urine Alb:Cr Detects albuminuria and hypertensive kidney damage. Albuminuria increases CV risk and favors an ACEi or ARB.
Urinalysis Screens for hematuria, proteinuria, glycosuria, and evidence suggesting intrinsic renal disease.
HbA1c or fasting glucose Detects diabetes or prediabetes, which substantially affects CV-risk assessment and treatment decisions.
Lipid panel Required for overall ASCVD-risk assessment and identification of a separate indication for lipid-lowering therapy.
CBC Detects anemia or erythrocytosis that may suggest CKD, hypoxemia, or another comorbidity.
TSH Screens for thyroid disease as a potentially reversible contributor to hypertension. Repeat only when clinically indicated.
12-lead ECG Assesses rhythm, prior MI, conduction disease, and possible LVH. Echocardiography is not routinely required unless the examination, symptoms, or ECG suggests structural heart disease.
Annual monitoring
BMP Monitor renal function and electrolytes, particularly with ACEi, ARB, MRA, or diuretic therapy. Check sooner after initiation or dose changes and more often with CKD or electrolyte abnormalities.
Urine Alb:Cr Repeat annually when CKD, diabetes, or prior albuminuria is present. In uncomplicated hypertension with a normal baseline result, the optimal repeat interval is not firmly established.
HbA1c or fasting glucose Repeat according to diabetes and prediabetes screening intervals; annually is reasonable with prediabetes or substantial metabolic risk. Hypertension alone does not necessarily require annual HbA1c.
Lipid panel Repeat according to ASCVD risk and lipid-management needs. Annual testing is useful during active treatment but is not mandatory for every patient with hypertension.
