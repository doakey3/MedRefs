Dementia - Diagnosis and Treatment


Mini-Cog

Physical Exam

  • Cogwheel rigidity
  • Hearing
  • Tremor
  • Gait abnormalities or slowing
  • Eye movement

Note: Alzheimer disease often shows no motor or sensory deficits


Laboratory Studies

  • B12 (everyone)
  • TSH (everyone)
  • Syphilis (high-risk sexual activity)
  • HIV (high-risk sexual activity)
  • Folic acid (alcoholism)
  • CMP, Serum calcium level (multiple myeloma, prostate cancer, breast cancer)

Functional Assessment

What can they no longer do independently that they could do a year or two ago because of their thinking or memory?

Has anyone had to start helping them with or taking over tasks because of their thinking or memory?

Dementia Types

Condition Typical Course Characteristic Clinical Features Typical MRI Findings
Alzheimer disease Slow, progressive decline over years Early prominent episodic memory impairment; repetitive questions; impaired learning of new information; later language, visuospatial, and executive dysfunction. Neurologic examination may initially be normal. Medial temporal/hippocampal and parietal-predominant atrophy, progressing to generalized cerebral atrophy.
Vascular cognitive impairment / vascular dementia Often stepwise decline after strokes, but may also progress gradually with extensive small-vessel disease Executive dysfunction and slowed processing often prominent; focal neurologic findings, gait impairment, or history of stroke; vascular risk factors common. Prior territorial or lacunar infarcts, white-matter hyperintensities, microhemorrhages, or other evidence of cerebrovascular disease.
Dementia with Lewy bodies Progressive over years, often with marked fluctuations Fluctuating cognition/attention, recurrent well-formed visual hallucinations, REM sleep behavior disorder, spontaneous parkinsonism. Memory impairment may be less prominent early. Often relatively nonspecific; relative preservation of medial temporal structures compared with Alzheimer disease.
Parkinson disease dementia Progressive; dementia develops after established Parkinson disease Parkinsonism precedes dementia by more than 1 year; executive and visuospatial dysfunction, slowed cognition, hallucinations may occur. Usually nonspecific cerebral atrophy; imaging is primarily useful to exclude other causes.
Behavioral-variant frontotemporal dementia Progressive over years; often presents younger than typical Alzheimer disease Early personality or behavioral change, disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, compulsive behavior, hyperorality, and executive dysfunction; memory may be relatively preserved early. Frontal and/or anterior temporal atrophy, frequently asymmetric.
Primary progressive aphasia Progressive language decline over years Language impairment is the dominant early deficit: word-finding, speech production, grammar, or word comprehension depending on subtype. Asymmetric dominant-hemisphere frontal, temporal, or temporoparietal atrophy depending on subtype.
Normal pressure hydrocephalus Usually progressive over months to years Gait impairment usually appears first and is most prominent; cognitive impairment and urinary urgency/incontinence follow. Ventriculomegaly disproportionate to cortical atrophy; enlarged ventricles without a corresponding degree of sulcal enlargement.
Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease Rapidly progressive over weeks to months Rapid cognitive decline with myoclonus, ataxia, behavioral change, visual symptoms, pyramidal or extrapyramidal findings. Diffusion restriction in cerebral cortex ("cortical ribboning") and/or basal ganglia, especially caudate and putamen.
Mixed dementia Variable; usually progressive Features of more than one process, commonly Alzheimer disease plus cerebrovascular disease. Combination of neurodegenerative atrophy and significant vascular abnormalities.

Dementia Severity

Severity Typical Functional Status
Mild Cognitive impairment is present, but the patient remains mostly independent. May need assistance with complex IADLs such as finances, medications, appointments, transportation, or other higher-level tasks.
Moderate Clearly requires regular assistance or supervision with everyday activities. Increasing difficulty with simpler tasks and may begin to require help with some basic ADLs.
Severe Dependent on others for basic ADLs and generally requires substantial supervision or caregiving.
