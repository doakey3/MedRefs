Diabetes Visit Checklist

  • Every visit
    • Weight / BMI
    • Blood pressure
    • Smoking status; counsel if applicable
    • Review glycemic control and medication adherence/tolerance
    • Assess for hypoglycemia
    • Check feet if neuropathy, PAD, ulcer history, or other foot concern
  • Every 3–6 months
    • A1c
  • Annually
    • Urine albumin/creatinine ratio
    • Renal function / creatinine
    • Comprehensive foot exam
    • Confirm diabetic eye exam is up to date
    • Review diabetes self-management / education needs
    • Review vaccinations
  • Periodically / as indicated
    • Lipid profile
    • Dental care / dental exam
    • Increase renal monitoring if CKD is present
    • Increase eye-exam frequency if retinopathy is present

Glycemic Control Targets

  • < 7%: healthy adults ≤ 64 yo
  • < 8%: patients > 65 yo and/or significant comorbidity: dementia, CKD 4/5 or ESRD, HF, ASCVD
  • < 9%: older adults w/multiple chronic conditions, significant cognitive deficits, or shortened life expectancy

Individualize A1c goal using shared decision-making.

Glucose Control Medications

First Line

  • Metformin XR — improved tolerance/adherence vs IR

Second Line

Choose based on patient-specific preferences, comorbidities, and goals.

  • Liraglutide (Victoza) — preferred GLP-1 RA
    • Mechanism(s): more insulin released from the pancreas when sugar is high; less sugar released from liver stores; reduces appetite; slows gastric emptying
    • Benefits patients w/ASCVD and proteinuria
    • Potential benefit in MASH and HF
    • Useful when weight loss is desirable
    • SQ daily
    • Titrate every 1–3 weeks: 0.6 mg → 1.2 mg → 1.8 mg; slower if GI side effects
    • Expected A1c reduction: ~0.7–1.5%
    • Expected weight loss: ~0.3–3.2 kg
    • If starting GLP-1 when A1c < 8% in a patient on insulin, reduce insulin by 20%
    • Stop any sulfonylurea when starting a GLP-1
    • Encourage contraception when using GLP-1's
    • Side effects:
      • nausea
      • vomiting
      • diarrhea
      • headache
      • injection-site reactions
    • Contraindications:
      • medullary thyroid cancer Hx or FMHx
      • MEN2
      • Type 1 DM
      • Hx of pancreatitis
      • Pregnancy/Pre-pregnancy
      • Breastfeeding
      • (Caution) Gastroparesis or severe GERD
      • (Caution) Risk of acute pancreatitis and gallbladder disease
  • Dapagliflozin (Farxiga) — preferred SGLT2 inhibitor
    • Mechanism(s): Allows kidneys to remove extra sugar into urine
    • Benefits patients w/ASCVD, CKD3, proteinuria, or HF
    • Cardiorenal benefits extend beyond glucose lowering
    • Check BMP ~1 month after initiation, every 6 to 12 months thereafter
    • 10 mg PO daily starting and maintenance dose for all indications
    • 5 mg may be considered if unable to tolerate 10 mg
    • A1c reduction ~0.5–0.7%
    • Weight loss ~2–3 kg
    • Modest BP reduction
    • Slows CKD progression
    • Reduces HF hospitalization
    • Reduces major cardiovascular events
    • Initial fall in eGFR / rise in creatinine is common and often partially resolves within ~3 months
    • Can initiate if eGFR > 20 mL/min/1.73 m²
    • If eGFR subsequently falls < 20, selected patients may continue therapy at discretion of Nephrology/Cardiology
    • If A1c < 8% or patient is prone to hypoglycemia, reduce sulfonylurea by 50%, insulin by 20%
    • Potential Side effects:
      • Genital mycotic infections
      • UTI
      • Dehydration / hypotension
      • Hypoglycemia, when combined w/other glucose-lowering medications
      • Euglycemic DKA
      • Fournier gangrene (rare)
    • Contraindications
      • eGFR < 20 mL/min/1.73 m² for initiation
      • Type 1 DM
      • Pregnancy
      • History of Fournier gangrene
  • Pioglitazone (Actos)
    • Mechanism(s): Improves sensitivity to insulin, but increases sodium retention in the kidneys
    • Potential benefit in MASH and major cardiovascular events
    • Slow onset — expect ~3-4 weeks before appreciable glycemic effect
    • Consider max 30 mg/day due to increasing fluid retention at higher doses (sodium retention at the kidneys)
    • Common adverse effects: weight gain, edema
    • Important adverse effects: HF exacerbation, fractures (especially women), macular edema; rare hepatotoxicity
    • Contraindicated in HF
    • Avoid w/macular edema, cirrhosis, osteoporosis, or bladder cancer
  • Sulfonylureas — glipizide or glimepiride
    • Mechanism(s): Increases insulin release from the pancreas (whether sugar is high or not)
    • Greater anticipated A1c reduction than SGLT2i or pioglitazone
    • May cause hypoglycemia. Avoid if high risk for severe hypoglycemia (alcoholism, erratic meals, hypoglycemia unawareness, or prior severe hypoglycemia)
    • Glipizide
      • Dose: 5 mg PO before breakfast; (consider 2.5 mg in elderly/frail patients or hepatic impairment); increase by 2.5-5 mg every several days based on glucose up to 40 mg/day
      • doses > 15 mg/day generally divided before meals
    • Glimepiride
      • Dose:1-2 mg PO daily with breakfast/first main meal; increased by 1-2 mg every 1-2 weeks up to 8 mg daily
      • Be very cautious in elderly and renally-impaired patients

If A1c is > 2% above goal or patient has overt hyperglycemia: start basal insulin.

Insulin

  • Preferred basal: insulin glargine-yfgn (Semglee)
    • New starts: place Diabetes Care Management referral; do NOT order insulin
    • If urgent start required: 10 units daily
    • Consider adding prandial insulin once basal dose exceeds 0.5 units/kg/day
  • Preferred prandial: insulin aspart-xjhz (Kirsty)
    • If A1c < 8%, consider decreasing basal insulin ~10% when adding prandial insulin
  • Insulin sig must contain units and specific dosing instructions, including correction scales.

Referrals / Injection Training

  • Diabetes Care Management: appropriate for DM2 patients w/A1c > 8% needing medication management
  • Injection training: patient fills injectable medication at pharmacy, then calls Kaiser to schedule nurse visit and brings medication to appointment
