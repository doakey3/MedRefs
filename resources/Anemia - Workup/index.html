Anemia - Workup


Initial Anemia Workup

Confirm anemia and compare with prior CBCs.

  • CBC with differential, MCV, RDW, RBC count, platelets
  • Reticulocyte count
  • Peripheral smear
  • Ferritin + iron/TIBC + calculated transferrin saturation (TSAT)
  • Creatinine/eGFR
  • Vitamin B12
  • Folate if macrocytosis, poor nutrition, alcohol use, malabsorption, or other risk factors
  • TSH
  • CMP/liver tests
  • CRP and/or ESR if inflammation is plausible

Interpret the Reticulocyte Response

  • Reticulocytes appropriately elevated: think blood loss, hemolysis, or recovery following treatment.
    • Look for overt or occult bleeding.
    • Hemolysis workup: LDH, indirect bilirubin, haptoglobin, smear ± DAT.
  • Reticulocytes low or inappropriately normal: think impaired RBC production.
    • Iron deficiency
    • Anemia of inflammation/chronic disease
    • CKD
    • B12/folate deficiency
    • Thalassemia/other hemoglobinopathy
    • Hypothyroidism
    • Medication/alcohol/liver disease
    • Bone marrow disease or malignancy
    • Unexplained anemia of aging

Microcytic Anemia

Think: iron deficiency, inflammation, or thalassemia first.

  • Ferritin low and TSAT low: iron deficiency.
    • Identify the cause rather than stopping at "iron deficiency."
    • Ask about menstrual bleeding, GI bleeding, blood donation, diet, bariatric surgery, and malabsorption.
    • Consider celiac disease and H. pylori when appropriate.
    • Men and postmenopausal women with unexplained IDA generally require GI evaluation.
  • Iron low + TSAT low + ferritin normal/high + TIBC low/normal: anemia of inflammation/chronic disease or CKD.
  • Persistent microcytosis with normal iron stores: consider thalassemia.
    • Often relatively high/normal RBC count despite low MCV.
    • Target cells may be present.
    • Order hemoglobin electrophoresis/HPLC.
    • Elevated HbA2 supports beta-thalassemia trait.
    • Alpha-thalassemia trait may have normal electrophoresis; genetic testing may be needed if confirmation matters.

Normocytic Anemia

Low/inappropriately normal reticulocytes:

  • Check iron studies: early or mixed iron deficiency may still be normocytic.
  • CKD:
    • Usually normocytic and hypoproliferative.
    • CKD becomes increasingly plausible as the cause as eGFR falls.
    • Do not automatically attribute anemia to CKD without excluding iron deficiency and other common causes.
    • Iron-restricted erythropoiesis is common in CKD and may produce low TSAT despite normal/high ferritin.
  • Anemia of inflammation/chronic disease:
    • Look for chronic inflammatory disease, infection, autoimmune disease, malignancy, or other inflammatory conditions.
    • Typical pattern: low serum iron, low/normal TIBC, low TSAT, normal/high ferritin.
    • CRP/ESR may support the presence of inflammation but does not establish the cause.
  • If unexplained: check B12, TSH, liver disease, medications, alcohol, hemolysis/blood loss if appropriate, and review the peripheral smear.

Macrocytic Anemia

  • B12 deficiency:
    • Check serum B12.
    • If B12 is borderline/indeterminate and suspicion remains, check methylmalonic acid (MMA).
    • Neurologic manifestations can occur even without dramatic anemia or macrocytosis.
    • Consider pernicious anemia, malabsorption, gastric surgery, metformin, and long-term acid suppression.
  • Folate deficiency:
    • Consider poor nutrition, alcohol use, malabsorption, or folate-antagonist medications.
    • Exclude B12 deficiency before treating presumed folate deficiency alone.
  • Also consider alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, medications, and reticulocytosis.
  • Unexplained macrocytosis, especially with cytopenias or abnormal smear: consider MDS or other marrow disease.

When to Think About Malignancy or Bone Marrow Disease

  • Progressive or otherwise unexplained anemia
  • More than one cytopenia
  • Abnormal WBC differential
  • Blasts, dysplastic cells, teardrop cells, or leukoerythroblastosis on smear
  • Unexplained macrocytosis
  • Constitutional symptoms, lymphadenopathy, or splenomegaly
  • Bone pain, hypercalcemia, renal dysfunction, or unexplained protein gap → consider SPEP/IFE/free light chains
  • Iron deficiency without an adequate explanation → evaluate for occult blood loss/GI malignancy as appropriate

Consider hematology referral and possible bone marrow evaluation when unexplained anemia is severe, progressive, associated with additional cytopenias, or accompanied by an abnormal peripheral smear.

Unexplained Anemia of Aging (UAA)

Diagnosis of exclusion. Usually a mild, isolated, hypoproliferative anemia in an older adult after reasonable exclusion of:

  • Iron deficiency or blood loss
  • B12/folate deficiency
  • CKD
  • Inflammatory disease
  • Thyroid disease
  • Hemolysis
  • Medication effects
  • Hematologic malignancy/MDS

TSAT = iron ÷ TIBC × 100

TSAT < 20% is low, less than 10% means iron deficiency highly likely.
