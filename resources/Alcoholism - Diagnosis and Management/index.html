Alcoholism - Diagnosis and Management

### Heavy Drinking [ ] Male who drinks greater than 14 drinks per week [ ] Male who drinks greater than 4 drinks per day [ ] Female who drinks greater than 7 drinks per week [ ] Female who drinks greater than 3 drinks per day ### Withdrawal Risk: [ ] Have you drunk daily or nearly daily for >4 weeks? [ ] Do you get withdrawal symptoms when you stop or cut down? [ ] Do you drink in the morning or to relieve withdrawal? [ ] Ever had withdrawal seizure, delerium tremens, hallucinations, or detox? ### Diagnostic criteria for alcohol use disorder (2-3: Mild; 4-5: Moderate; ≥6: Severe) [ ] Uses more or longer than intended [ ] Wants or tries unsuccessfully to cut down [ ] Spends substantial time obtaining, using, or recovering [ ] Craving or strong urge to drink [ ] Repeatedly fails major work, school, or home obligations [ ] Continues despite recurrent social or interpersonal problems [ ] Reduces or abandons work/recreational activities because of alcohol [ ] Repeatedly uses alcohol in physically hazardous situations [ ] Continues despite known physical or psychological harm [ ] Tolerance: needs more, or the same amount has less effect [ ] Withdrawal, or drinks/uses a sedative to prevent withdrawal ### Contraindications to ambulatory alcohol withdrawal [ ] Prior alcohol-withdrawal seizure or delirium tremens [ ] Hx of CIWA-Ar >15 [ ] NYHA class II-IV heart failure (Stairs, Walk, Rest) [ ] Decompensated cirrhosis [ ] Oxygen-dependent COPD [ ] Stage 4-5 chronic kidney disease [ ] Epilepsy or any prior seizure [ ] Recent head injury with LOC or intracranial bleeding [ ] Active psychosis, mania, severe depression, or suicidality [ ] Acute febrile illness [ ] Current or prior benzodiazepine use disorder [ ] Pregnancy [ ] Cannot reliably assess or report symptoms [ ] Cannot reliably follow the medication regimen [ ] Cannot complete daily follow-up [ ] Cognitive impairment without reliable support [ ] No reliable contact, transportation, or remote access

Sustained Remission: No criteria met for diagnosis in the past 12 months or longer

Medications To Facilitate Abstinence

Medication Best use Advantages Disadvantages / avoid
Naltrexone
50 mg daily		 Usually first-line.
Best for reducing heavy drinking, craving, or relapse.
  • Abstinence not required
  • Once-daily dosing
  • Monthly injection available
  • Strongest practical first choice
  • Cannot use with opioids
  • May precipitate opioid withdrawal
  • Avoid in acute hepatitis or advanced liver disease
  • Nausea, headache, or dysphoria
Acamprosate
666 mg three times daily		 Maintaining abstinence after withdrawal.
Preferred when significant liver disease precludes naltrexone.
  • No hepatic metabolism
  • No opioid interaction
  • Can continue if relapse occurs
  • Three-times-daily dosing
  • Works best after abstinence begins
  • Diarrhea is common
  • Reduce dose if CrCl 30–50
  • Contraindicated if CrCl ≤30
Topiramate
Titrate gradually		 Off-label alternative when naltrexone is unsuitable.
Attractive with obesity, migraines, or seizures.
  • Reduces heavy drinking and craving
  • Abstinence not required
  • May promote weight loss
  • Also treats migraine and seizures
  • Off-label for alcohol use disorder
  • Cognitive slowing and word-finding problems
  • Paresthesias and taste changes
  • Kidney stones and metabolic acidosis
  • Teratogenic; adjust for renal impairment
Disulfiram
250 mg daily		 Selected, highly motivated patient committed to abstinence.
Most useful when dosing is supervised.
  • Once-daily dosing
  • Creates a strong external deterrent
  • No renal dose adjustment
  • Does not reduce craving
  • Effectiveness depends heavily on adherence
  • Alcohol reaction can be severe
  • Hepatotoxicity and neuropathy
  • Many hidden alcohol exposures and interactions
  • Avoid with psychosis, severe heart disease, or metronidazole

Outpatient Withdrawal Regimens

Very mild withdrawal (CIWA-Ar <10)
  • Thiamine 100 mg daily
  • folic acid 400 mcg–1 mg daily
  • Gabapentin fixed taper:
    • Day 1: 300 mg every 6 hours
    • Day 2: 300 mg every 8 hours
    • Day 3: 300 mg every 12 hours
    • Day 4: 300 mg at night
  • Gabapentin 300 mg once daily as needed for symptoms
  • Use a benzodiazepine taper if already taking gabapentin
  • Adjust gabapentin dose for CKD

Mild withdrawal (CIWA-Ar 10–15)

Option 1

  • Thiamine 100 mg daily
  • folic acid 400 mcg–1 mg daily
  • Diazepam fixed taper:
    • Day 1: 10 mg every 6 hours
    • Day 2: 10 mg every 8 hours
    • Day 3: 10 mg every 12 hours
    • Day 4: 10 mg at night
  • Diazepam 10 mg once daily as needed for symptoms

Option 2

  • Chlordiazepoxide fixed taper:
    • Day 1: 50 mg every 6 hours
    • Day 2: 50 mg every 8 hours
    • Day 3: 50 mg every 12 hours
    • Day 4: 50 mg at night
  • Chlordiazepoxide 50 mg once daily as needed for symptoms
