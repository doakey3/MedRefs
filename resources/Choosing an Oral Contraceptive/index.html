Choosing an Oral Contraceptive


Reasonably Certain of Non-Pregnancy

If there are no signs/symptoms of pregnancy, pregnancy can be reasonably excluded if any one is true:

  • ≤7 days after the start of normal menses
  • No intercourse since the start of the last normal menses
  • Correct and consistent use of a reliable contraceptive method
  • ≤7 days after spontaneous or induced abortion
  • Within 4 weeks postpartum
  • Fully or nearly fully breastfeeding (≥85% of feeds), amenorrheic, AND <6 months postpartum

Urine pregnancy testing: Optional if one of the above criteria is met. If none is met, a negative test does not reliably exclude an early pregnancy resulting from recent intercourse.

Contraindications to Using Combined Oral Contraceptives

CDC U.S. Medical Eligibility Criteria categories 3 and 4:

  • History of breast cancer
  • Less than 42 days postpartum
  • Severe liver cirrhosis
  • Personal history of DVT or PE
  • Recent major surgery with prolonged immobility
  • Diabetes mellitus with nephropathy, neuropathy, or retinopathy
  • Vascular disease of >20 years' duration
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Migraines with aura
  • History of bariatric surgery with risk of malabsorption
  • History of cholestasis related to a combined oral contraceptive
  • Inflammatory bowel disease
  • Heart disease
  • Known thrombogenic mutation (e.g., Factor V Leiden)
  • Liver tumor (hepatocellular adenoma or malignant)
  • Hypertension
  • Multiple risk factors for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease:
    • Older than 35
    • Smoking
    • Diabetes
    • Low HDL
    • High LDL
    • High triglycerides
  • Multiple sclerosis with prolonged immobility
  • Complicated solid organ transplant recipient
  • Systemic lupus erythematosus
  • Using antiretroviral, anticonvulsant, rifampin, or rifabutin therapy

Emergency Contraception

Situation Options / notes
Unprotected intercourse ≤5 days ago Consider copper IUD, ulipristal 30 mg, or levonorgestrel 1.5 mg.
3–5 days after intercourse Ulipristal is more effective than levonorgestrel.
Obesity Levonorgestrel emergency contraception may be less effective; ulipristal or copper IUD is preferred when feasible.
After levonorgestrel EC Regular hormonal contraception may be started immediately.
After ulipristal EC Generally wait 5 days before starting hormonal contraception, then use condoms/abstinence for 7 days after starting.

Choosing a Method: Practical Fit

Method Particularly useful when... Important tradeoffs
Copper IUD Wants no hormones, minimal maintenance, or highly effective emergency contraception May increase menstrual bleeding and cramping
LNG-IUD Wants minimal maintenance, lighter periods, menstrual suppression, dysmenorrhea, or heavy menstrual bleeding Insertion procedure; irregular bleeding is common initially
Etonogestrel implant Wants minimal maintenance without a pelvic procedure Bleeding pattern is unpredictable
DMPA Wants an infrequent method and/or eventual amenorrhea Irregular bleeding initially; weight gain and reversible BMD loss are considerations
Combined pill / patch / ring Wants predictable cycles, menstrual suppression, acne benefit, or treatment of dysmenorrhea Requires ongoing adherence and must be appropriate for estrogen use
Progestin-only pill Wants a pill but estrogen is undesirable or contraindicated Irregular bleeding; missed-pill and backup rules vary by formulation

Noncontraceptive Goals

Predictable cyclic bleeding Combined pill, patch, or ring

Starting Today and Back-Up Contraception

Method No backup needed if started... Otherwise Required before initiation
Copper IUD Any time pregnancy is reasonably excluded No backup Cervical inspection
LNG-IUD ≤7 days after menses began Backup 7 days Cervical inspection
Implant ≤5 days after menses began Backup 7 days None routinely
DMPA ≤7 days after menses began Backup 7 days None routinely
Combined pill / patch / ring ≤5 days after menses began Backup 7 days Blood pressure
Norethindrone / norgestrel POP ≤5 days after menses began Backup 2 days None routinely
Drospirenone POP Day 1 of menstrual bleeding Backup 7 days None routinely*

If pregnancy status is uncertain: pill, patch, ring, DMPA, implant, and POP may generally be quick-started with a repeat pregnancy test in 2–4 weeks. Do not place an IUD until pregnancy can be reasonably excluded.

Common Oral Contraceptives

  • Levonorgestrel 0.1 mg / ethinyl estradiol 20 mcg — good default combined OCP.
  • Levonorgestrel 0.15 mg / ethinyl estradiol 30 mcg — consider if bothersome breakthrough bleeding on a 20-mcg pill.
  • Drospirenone 3 mg / ethinyl estradiol 20 mcg (24/4) — particularly useful for acne or PMDD.
  • Norethindrone 0.35 mg — traditional progestin-only pill.
  • Drospirenone 4 mg (24/4) — progestin-only pill with a more forgiving missed-pill window.
