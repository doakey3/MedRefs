Celiac Disease

Indications For Celiac Testing

  • Any first-degree relative of a person with confirmed celiac disease
  • Chronic (3 months or more) gastrointestinal complaint not explained by another process
    • Diarrhea/steatorrhea (fatty/oily stools)
    • Constipation
    • Unexpected weight loss (5% or more in a 6-month period)
    • Abdominal pain
    • Bloating/distension
  • Type 1 Diabetes
  • Autoimmune Thyroiditis
  • Down syndrome
  • Turner Syndrome
  • Pulmonary Hemosiderosis
  • Dermatitis herpetiformis
  • Unexplained anemia
  • Unexplained B12/folate deficiency
  • Unexplained fatigue
  • Persistent aphthous stomatitis
  • Persistently elevated serum aminotransferases
  • Recurrent headaches
  • Recurrent fetal loss
  • Low birth weight offspring
  • Reduced fertility
  • Premature osteoporosis
  • Idiopathic peripheral neuropathy
  • Nonhereditary cerebellar ataxia

Test What It Measures Clinical Use Interpretation
tTG-IgA IgA antibodies against tissue transglutaminase, an enzyme involved in protein modification. Preferred initial screening test for celiac disease. Positive suggests celiac disease. Negative makes celiac less likely if the patient consumes gluten and has sufficient IgA.
Total IgA Total circulating immunoglobulin A, regardless of antigen specificity. Detects IgA deficiency, which can cause falsely negative tTG-IgA results. Low or undetectable IgA warrants IgG-based testing.
DGP-IgG IgG antibodies against deamidated gliadin peptides (chemically modified fragments of gluten). Alternative test when IgA deficiency is present. Positive supports celiac disease; negative reduces its likelihood but does not exclude it.
tTG-IgG IgG antibodies against tissue transglutaminase. Alternative test when IgA deficiency is present. Positive supports celiac disease in IgA-deficient patients. Less useful when total IgA is normal.
EMA-IgA IgA anti-endomysial antibodies, which recognize tissue transglutaminase. Highly specific confirmatory serology, especially when tTG-IgA results are equivocal. Positive strongly supports celiac disease. Limited by IgA deficiency and reduced gluten exposure.
HLA-DQ2 Genetic variants encoding HLA-DQ2 antigen-presenting molecules, particularly DQ2.5 and DQ2.2. Assesses genetic susceptibility, particularly in patients already avoiding gluten or with inconclusive testing. Negative reduces the likelihood of celiac disease; positive indicates susceptibility, not disease.
HLA-DQ8 Genetic variants encoding the HLA-DQ8 antigen-presenting molecule. Assesses genetic susceptibility alongside HLA-DQ2. Absence of both HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 makes celiac disease extremely unlikely (>99% negative predictive value).
