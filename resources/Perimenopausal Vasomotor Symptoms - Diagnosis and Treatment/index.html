Perimenopausal Vasomotor Symptoms - Diagnosis and Treatment

### When taking hormone therapy... - It may help to improve menopausal symptoms like hot flash - It reduces the risk of fracturing a bone by a little bit - It increases the risk of breast cancer by a little bit - Other nonhormonal options may reduce hot flashes, but not as well as hormone therapy - Spotting is common during the first 3-6 months of treatment - Menopausal hormone therapy does not provide contraception

Atrophic Vaginitis Symptoms

  • Vaginal dryness
  • Vaginal burning
  • Dyspareunia
  • Loss of vaginal secretions
  • Leukorrhea (clear or milky vaginal discharge)
  • Vulvar pruritis
  • Feeling of vaginal pressure
  • Vaginal itching with yellow malodorous discharge

Contraindications

  • Breast cancer or other estrogen-sensitive malignancy
  • Venous thromboembolism
  • Stroke
  • Coronary artery disease
  • Active liver disease
  • Uncontrolled hypertension
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding

Relative Contraindications

  • Migraine with aura
  • Controlled hypertension
  • Hypertriglyceridemia
  • Strong family history of hormone-dependent cancer
  • High cardiovascular risk
  • High venous thromboembolism risk

Laboratory Studies

  • Age <40: Evaluate for primary ovarian insufficiency with FSH.
  • Age 40–44: Consider FSH to support early menopause.
  • Age ≥45: Diagnose typical perimenopause or menopause clinically; do not routinely obtain FSH or estradiol.
  • TSH if thyroid disease suspected
  • Prolactin for unexplained amenorrhea, galactorrhea, headache, or visual symptoms.
  • CBC ± ferritin (if heavy or prolonged uterine bleeding)

Nonhormonal Treatment of Vasomotor Symptoms

  • Neurokinin-receptor antagonists
    • Elinzanetant 120 mg PO nightly
    • Fezolinetant 45 mg PO daily (requires baseline and follow-up hepatic panels)
  • Gabapentin
    • Start 100–300 mg PO nightly; titrate toward 300 mg three times daily
    • Especially useful for night sweats, insomnia, or neuropathic pain
  • Oxybutynin
    • 2.5–5 mg PO twice daily or ER 5–15 mg daily
    • Can be highly effective but is limited by anticholinergic adverse effects (esp dry mouth)
  • SNRIs
    • Venlafaxine XR 37.5 mg daily x7 days, then increase to 75 mg daily; taper when stopping
    • Desvenlafaxine 50–100 mg daily
    • Useful when depression, anxiety, or neuropathic pain is also present
  • SSRIs
    • Escitalopram 10 mg daily; increase to 20 mg daily
    • Citalopram 10–20 mg daily
  • Behavioral interventions
    • Cognitive behavioral therapy
    • Clinical hypnosis
    • Primarily reduce symptom burden and interference rather than hot-flash frequency
  • Weight loss (may modestly improve vasomotor symptoms in patients with overweight or obesity)
