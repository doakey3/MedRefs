International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS)

Physical Exam

  • Suprapubic and genital region (STI? Phimosis? Urethral stricture? Cancer masses? Bladder enlargement?)
  • Cardiovascular problems: Hypotension? Aortic stenosis? Lower extremity edema?
  • Neurologic exam. Sensory deficits?
  • Digital rectal exam. Is the prostate huge or severely abnormal?

Red Flags

  • PSA ≥ 4 ng/mL
  • History of Prostate Cancer
  • Recurrent urinary tract infections
  • Bladder stone
  • Hydronephrosis
  • Refractory urinary retention
  • Gross hematuria
  • Progressive renal impairment
  • Very abnormal prostate on physical exam

Lifestyle Modifications

  • Strategic fluid intake
  • Maintain physical activity
  • Timed voiding
  • Double voiding

Tamsulosin

  • Mechanism: Relaxes smooth muscles in the prostate and bladder neck → lower outlet resistance
  • Dose: 0.4 mg PO daily ~30 mins after the same meal each day; increase to 0.8 mg PO daily after 2-4 weeks if inadequate control
  • If therapy is interrupted for several days, start at 0.4 mg even if previously taking 0.8 mg. Continue x 2 weeks, then go back to 0.8 mg
  • Side effects:
    • Orthostatic hypotension, falls. Worse when combined with antihypertensives and PDE-5i (sildenafil, tadalafil)
    • Ejaculatory dysfunction
    • Intraoperatie floppy iris syndrome -- ask about planned cataract or glaucoma surgery, the ophthalmologist needs to know

Finasteride

  • Mechanism: decreases conversion of testosterone → DHT → gradual shrinkage (~20%) of prostate over about 6-12 months.
  • Dose: 5 mg PO daily
  • Pregnant women should not handle crushed or broken finasteride tablets
  • Finasteride will lower the PSA by 50% after 6 months. Establish a new baseline PSA at that time.
  • Side Effects:
    • Decreased libido
    • Erectile dysfunction
    • Ejaculatory dysfunction
    • Gynecomastia
    • Decreased fertility
