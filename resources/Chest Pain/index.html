Outpatient Chest Pain

ECG Findings Requiring Emergent Action

ECG Pattern Key Findings
Standard STEMI New ST elevation at the J point in ≥2 contiguous leads:
  • All leads except V2–V3: ≥1 mm
  • V2–V3, men ≥40: ≥2 mm
  • V2–V3, men <40: ≥2.5 mm
  • V2–V3, women: ≥1.5 mm
Posterior MI Horizontal ST depression V1–V3, often with tall R waves and upright T waves. Posterior leads V7–V9 may show ST elevation ≥0.5 mm.
LBBB / Paced Rhythm with Positive Modified Sgarbossa Any of:
  • Concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm
  • Concordant ST depression ≥1 mm in V1–V3
  • Discordant ST elevation ≥1 mm with ST/S-wave ratio ≥0.25
De Winter Pattern Upsloping ST depression at the J point in precordial leads with tall, symmetric T waves; often associated with proximal LAD occlusion.
Hyperacute T Waves New, broad-based, disproportionately tall regional T waves, especially with reciprocal ST depression or dynamic changes.
Subtle Occlusion MI Regional ST elevation below conventional STEMI thresholds with convincing reciprocal ST depression or evolving ischemic changes.

ECG Findings Requiring Urgent Evaluation, Ischemic Changes

ECG Pattern Key Findings Action
ST Depression New horizontal or downsloping ST depression ≥0.5 mm in ≥2 contiguous leads Evaluate for NSTEMI/unstable angina; serial ECGs and troponins
T-Wave Inversion New, symmetric T-wave inversion ≥1 mm in ≥2 contiguous leads Evaluate for ischemia; compare prior ECGs and obtain serial troponins
Wellens Pattern Deeply inverted or biphasic T waves in V2–V3, often after chest pain resolves Urgent cardiology consultation; avoid stress testing
Diffuse ST Depression + aVR Elevation Widespread ST depression with ST elevation in aVR, suggesting global subendocardial ischemia Urgent assessment; early cardiology consultation, especially with ongoing pain or instability
New LBBB New or presumed-new LBBB without modified Sgarbossa criteria Evaluate for ACS based on symptoms, serial ECGs, and troponins
Nonspecific ST-T Changes Mild ST deviation, T-wave flattening, or nonspecific T-wave inversion Compare prior ECGs; evaluate according to clinical risk
Normal ECG No acute ischemic abnormalities Does not exclude ACS; use validated hs-troponin pathway if suspected

LIFE-THREATENING CONDITIONS
Acute coronary syndromeRest pain, new or worsening angina; may present with dyspnea, nausea, weakness, or syncope.ED/EMS; ECG, serial troponins, ACS protocol.
Pulmonary embolismAcute dyspnea, pleuritic pain, tachypnea, cough, or unilateral leg swelling.ED; Wells/PERC, D-dimer or CTPA; anticoagulation if confirmed or strongly suspected.
Dangerous arrhythmiaVT or complete heart block; palpitations, dyspnea, presyncope, or syncope.ED/EMS; ECG, cardiac monitoring, ACLS if unstable.
Acute aortic syndromeSudden severe chest/back pain; possible pulse deficit, syncope, or neurological deficits.EMS; CTA aorta, IV anti-impulse therapy, urgent surgical consultation.
Tension pneumothoraxSudden unilateral pleuritic pain, respiratory distress, hypotension.Immediate decompression; emergency chest drainage.
Cardiac tamponadeHypotension, tachycardia, elevated JVP; possible muffled heart sounds.ED/EMS; urgent echocardiogram and pericardial drainage if unstable.
Esophageal ruptureSevere chest pain after vomiting; possible subcutaneous emphysema or sepsis.ED; CT imaging, NPO, IV antibiotics, urgent surgical consultation.
Perforated peptic ulcerSudden severe chest/abdominal pain, abdominal rigidity.ED; CT abdomen, NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics, urgent surgery.
Takotsubo cardiomyopathyMI-like chest pain after emotional or physical stress; transient LV dysfunction.ED; exclude ACS, echocardiogram, cardiology consultation.
CARDIAC
Stable anginaPredictable exertional pressure relieved by rest or nitroglycerin.ECG, assess CAD probability; CCTA or stress imaging if indicated; optimize CAD therapy.
Heart failureChest discomfort with dyspnea, fatigue, edema, or orthopnea.BNP/NT-proBNP, ECG, echocardiogram; diuretics if congested, initiate GDMT.
Pericarditis / myopericarditisSharp pleuritic pain relieved by leaning forward; possible diffuse ST elevation.ECG, troponin, CRP, echocardiogram; NSAID + colchicine for uncomplicated pericarditis; urgent evaluation if myocardial involvement.
MyocarditisChest pain, palpitations, presyncope, or new heart failure symptoms.Urgent evaluation; ECG, troponin, echocardiogram; cardiology, consider cardiac MRI.
Aortic stenosisExertional chest pain, dyspnea, or syncope; systolic murmur.Echocardiogram; cardiology referral if symptomatic or significant stenosis.
Mitral stenosisProgressive exertional dyspnea, sometimes angina-like pain; diastolic murmur.Echocardiogram; cardiology referral.
GASTROINTESTINAL
GERDPostprandial or nocturnal retrosternal burning, regurgitation.Empiric PPI for 4–8 weeks; lifestyle modifications; EGD if alarm features.
Peptic ulcer diseaseEpigastric pain, nausea, early satiety, food-associated symptoms.H. pylori testing/treatment, PPI, avoid NSAIDs; EGD if indicated.
Esophageal motility disorderSpasmodic substernal pain, often after meals; possible dysphagia.GI referral; EGD and esophageal manometry as indicated.
EsophagitisRetrosternal pain with odynophagia; infectious or medication-related.Remove offending medication; PPI for reflux; EGD or targeted antimicrobials if indicated.
Eosinophilic esophagitisDysphagia, food impaction, refractory heartburn.GI referral, EGD with biopsies; PPI or swallowed topical corticosteroid.
Hiatal herniaChest discomfort with reflux symptoms.EGD; GERD treatment; consider GI/surgical referral for large or complicated hernia.
PULMONARY
PneumothoraxSudden unilateral pleuritic pain and dyspnea; reduced breath sounds.Urgent chest X-ray or ultrasound; observation, aspiration, or drainage depending on severity.
PneumoniaPleuritic pain, fever, productive cough, focal lung findings.Chest X-ray; antibiotics if bacterial; assess need for hospitalization.
Lung cancerPersistent chest pain, cough, hemoptysis, weight loss.Chest CT; pulmonology referral for tissue diagnosis.
Asthma / COPDChest tightness, wheezing, dyspnea.Bronchodilators; spirometry when stable; treat exacerbation as indicated.
PleuritisPleuritic pain associated with infection, autoimmune disease, or medications.Evaluate underlying cause; NSAID if appropriate.
SarcoidosisCough, dyspnea, occasionally pleuritic pain or arrhythmias.Chest imaging, ECG; pulmonology referral; corticosteroids if clinically indicated.
Acute chest syndrome (sickle cell)Chest pain, fever, hypoxemia, new pulmonary infiltrate.ED/admission; oxygen if hypoxemic, antibiotics, analgesia, consider transfusion.
Pulmonary hypertensionExertional dyspnea, chest pain, syncope.Echocardiogram; referral to pulmonary hypertension specialist; right-heart catheterization for confirmation.
MUSCULOSKELETAL
Costochondritis / chest wall painLocalized reproducible tenderness, pain with movement.Topical or oral NSAID if appropriate; reassurance, activity modification.
Rib fractureFocal reproducible pain, usually following trauma.Analgesia, pulmonary hygiene; imaging if complications suspected.
Rheumatic disease / fibromyalgiaChest wall pain with inflammatory joint symptoms or widespread tenderness.Evaluate underlying disorder; targeted anti-inflammatory treatment or fibromyalgia management.
Chest wall traumaLocalized pain following thoracic injury.Assess for fracture or intrathoracic injury; analgesia and supportive care.
PSYCHIATRIC
Panic attack / disorderEpisodic chest pain, palpitations, sweating, dyspnea, paresthesias, intense fear.Assess for panic disorder; CBT, SSRI/SNRI if indicated.
Other psychiatric conditionsDepression or somatic symptom disorder associated with chest discomfort.Screen for psychiatric disorders; psychotherapy and condition-specific treatment.
COCAINE AND METHAMPHETAMINE
Cocaine / methamphetamine useAcute chest pain from vasospasm, ischemia, arrhythmias, or dissection.ED for acute symptoms; ECG/troponins, supportive care; treat stimulant use disorder.
REFERRED PAIN
Referred abdominal painCholecystitis or pancreatitis causing chest discomfort.Lipase, liver tests, abdominal ultrasound or CT as indicated; treat underlying cause.
Referred spinal painChest pain associated with cervical/thoracic spine movement or radicular symptoms.Conservative treatment, physical therapy; imaging if neurological deficits or red flags.
HERPES ZOSTER
Herpes zosterUnilateral dermatomal pain or dysesthesia, often preceding vesicular rash.Valacyclovir 1 g PO TID for 7 days (renal-adjust); analgesia.
INTIMATE PARTNER VIOLENCE
Intimate partner violenceChest pain associated with trauma, anxiety, or psychological distress.Private assessment, injury evaluation, safety planning, social work/resources.

Outpatient

Call 911 If (<30 sec assessment)

  • Severe dyspnea, respiratory distress, or hypoxemia
  • Diaphoresis, pallor, or cool/clammy skin with acute chest pain
  • Severe or persistent chest pain with obvious distress
  • Hypotension, weak pulse, or signs of shock
  • Syncope, altered mental status, or new focal neurological deficit
  • Sudden, maximal-at-onset chest or back pain (suspect aortic dissection)
  • Chest pain with sustained marked tachycardia, bradycardia, or an irregular pulse causing symptoms

Send to ED If (2-Minute Assessment)

  • New or persistent chest pressure/tightness at rest
  • New or worsening exertional chest pain
  • Chest pain with radiation to arm/jaw, dyspnea, nausea, or presyncope concerning for ACS
  • Pleuritic pain or unexplained dyspnea with PE risk factors, tachycardia, or unilateral leg swelling
  • Sudden chest/back pain with known aortic aneurysm or pulse asymmetry
  • Acute chest pain after cocaine/methamphetamine use
  • Persistent unexplained pain with significant concern for a dangerous cause
