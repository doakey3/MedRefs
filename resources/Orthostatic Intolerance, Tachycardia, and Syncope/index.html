Red Flags

  • Exertional syncope (structural heart disease or arrhythmia)
  • Supine syncope (arrhythmia)
  • Abrupt syncope without prodrome (arrhythmia)
  • Palpitations immediately preceding syncope (arrhythmia)
  • Known structural heart disease or heart failure (cardiogenic)
  • Abnormal cardiac examination (structural heart disease)
  • Abnormal ECG (arrhythmia or conduction disease)
  • Family history of premature sudden unexplained death (inherited arrhythmia or cardiomyopathy)
  • Chest pain or significant dyspnea (acute cardiopulmonary disease)
  • Hypoxemia (cardiopulmonary disease)
  • Evidence of severe anemia or active bleeding (hypovolemia or impaired oxygen delivery)
  • Focal neurologic deficit or persistent altered mental status (not uncomplicated syncope)
  • Prolonged convulsive activity or delayed recovery (seizure or other neurologic cause)

Key History

  • Dehydration or inadequate fluid intake
  • Blood loss or anemia
  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Fever or infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Hyperthyroidism
  • Hypoxemia or cardiopulmonary disease
  • Adrenal insufficiency
  • Recent substantial weight loss
  • Prolonged bed rest or physical deconditioning
  • Alcohol or other substance use
  • Pain, anxiety, panic, or hyperventilation

Pharmacologic Causes

  • Diuretics
  • Alpha-1 blockers
  • Nitrates and other potent vasodilators
  • Centrally acting alpha-2 agonists
  • Beta-blockers and non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers
  • Antipsychotics and tricyclic antidepressants
  • Sedative-hypnotics and opioids
  • Dopaminergic medications
  • Stimulants, sympathomimetics, atomoxetine, and SNRIs

Note: Not all-inclusive. Evaluate timing, dose changes, patient's age, volume status.

Essential Physical Exam

  • Cardiac and lung auscultation
  • Volume status and lower-extremity edema
  • Peripheral pulses and perfusion
  • Thyroid examination
  • Focused neurologic examination

Labs

Essential Labs

  • CBC
  • BMP
  • TSH

Case-specific Labs

  • Pregnancy
  • Morning cortisol
  • Vitamin B12
  • Hemoglobin a1c
  • Iron studies + Ferritin

10-Minute Active Stand Test

Stop test immediately if patient is unable to complete the test safely

  1. Patient supine quietly x 5 mins
  2. Record supine HR & BP
  3. Patient stands upright without walking, leaning, or contracting the leg muscles unnecessarily.
  4. Record HR, BP, & symptoms after standing for 3 minutes.
  5. If systolic ↓ ≥20 OR diastolic ↓ ≥10, STOP
  6. Else, continue monitoring HR while the patient remains standing for a total of 10 minutes.
  7. If HR ↑ ≥30 beats/min above baseline, confirm whether it remains that high x ≥1 min.
  8. At 10 minutes, record HR, BP, and symptoms again.

Remain close enough to prevent injury if the patient becomes presyncopal. When feasible, perform testing in the morning because orthostatic tachycardia may be more pronounced earlier in the day.

Orthostatic Hypotension Treatment

  • Ambulatory BP monitoring if in-clinic supine BP is elevated
  • Correct underlying cause and adjust causative medications
  • Increase fluid and sodium intake if appropriate
  • Avoid triggers
  • Use counterpressure maneuvers (leg-crossing, squatting)
  • Exercise
  • Waist-high compression
  • If persistent: midodrine or droxidopa
  • If refractory: consider adjunctive therapy or specialty referral

POTS Treatment

  • Correct contributing conditions
  • Adjust causative medications
  • ↑ fluid to 3 L/d if appropriate
  • ↑ sodium intake to 3.2 to 4.8 g of sodium/d if appropriate
  • Avoid triggers and prolonged inactivity
  • Use waist-high compression garments
  • Exercise. Begin w/semi-recumbent exercises: row, swim, recumbent-bike
  • If persistent: consider propranolol, ivabradine, or midodrine based on HR and BP
  • If refractory: consider additional therapy or specialty referral
